How to Track Your Snake's Feedings: The Complete Guide

If you've ever asked yourself "when did my snake last eat?" and couldn't answer with certainty, you're not alone. It's one of the most common struggles among reptile keepers, especially once you're managing more than one animal.

Why Tracking Feedings Matters

A snake skipping a meal isn't always a problem — many species, pythons in particular, can refuse food for extended periods during shedding, breeding season, or simply due to environmental stress. But without an accurate history, it's impossible to tell a normal fast from a worrying one.

Knowing for certain:

  • When the last meal was
  • What kind of prey it ate (and what weight)
  • Whether the meal was regurgitated

...lets you step in early if something's off, instead of noticing weeks later.

What to Record for Every Feeding

A complete feeding log should include at least:

  1. Date of the meal
  2. Prey type (mouse, rat, chick, etc.) and whether live, frozen/thawed
  3. Prey weight — useful for tracking the prey-to-snake weight ratio over time
  4. Quantity (some snakes, especially large adults, eat more than one prey item per meal)
  5. Notes — behavior during feeding, any regurgitation, observed digestion time

The Problem with Notebooks and Spreadsheets

The most common method is still a paper notebook or a spreadsheet. It works, up to a point — then it quickly becomes unmanageable:

  • No automatic alerts: if a snake hasn't eaten in 40 days, nothing tells you — you have to remember or scroll through rows manually
  • Hard to check on the spot: if you're in front of the enclosure and want to know the last feeding date, you need to open a file on another device
  • No overview: understanding trends over time (feeding frequency, prey weight growth) means building charts and pivot tables by hand

Fine for a single animal. For a collection with multiple snakes, the risk of losing track grows with every new animal.

How to Set Up a Snake Breeding Log (Notebook, Excel, or App)

Sooner or later, every snake keeper faces the same question: how do you keep track of everything in an organized way? The answer depends on how many snakes you have and how much time you want to spend managing data instead of the animals themselves.

The Three Most Common Methods

1. Paper Notebook

It's the starting point for almost everyone. It has real advantages: zero learning curve, no dependence on battery or connection, and you can jot something down in seconds in front of the enclosure.

The limits show up as the collection grows:

  • Impossible to search information quickly ("when did *that* snake last eat?" means flipping through pages)
  • No way to see trends over time without rebuilding them by hand
  • Physical risk of loss, damage, or illegible handwriting

Works well for up to 2-3 snakes followed closely on a daily basis. Beyond that, the time spent searching for information starts to add up.

2. Excel / Google Sheets

The natural next step: structured, searchable data that you can sort and filter. For anyone comfortable with spreadsheets, it also allows building charts and pivot tables.

The problem is that it requires maintenance: every useful feature (alerts for missed meals, a growth chart, reminders) has to be built by hand, and rebuilt or adapted every time your data structure changes. On top of that:

  • Not designed for quick checks from your phone in front of the enclosure
  • Doesn't send automatic notifications — if a snake hasn't eaten in a month, the sheet won't tell you, you have to check yourself
  • With multiple collaborators (e.g. a family-run collection), version management gets messy

3. A Dedicated App

Built specifically to solve the limits of the first two methods. The useful features (alerts, charts, history) are already there — nothing to build. The typical downsides are the potential cost and the time needed to migrate existing data — which is often why it only makes sense to switch once the size of your collection justifies it.

When It's Worth Switching Tools

There's no universal threshold, but a few signs point to the right moment:

  • It takes you more than a few minutes to answer "when did this snake last eat?"
  • You've noticed a prolonged fast or a missed shed too late
  • You're managing more than 3-4 snakes and the notebook/sheet takes more time than you'd like
  • You want to share an animal's history with a vet quickly and clearly

What to Look for in an App, If You Decide to Switch

The features that really make a difference compared to a homemade spreadsheet:

  • Automatic alerts for missed meals or overdue sheds
  • Ready-made charts (weight growth curve) with nothing to build
  • Quick access from your phone, even offline
  • PDF export to share a complete profile with your vet
  • A free plan to try it out on a few animals before committing

In Short

There's no single wrong method — it depends on how many animals you're tracking and how much time you want to spend on the "administrative" side of keeping them. A notebook is perfectly fine to start with. When it starts costing you more time than it saves, that's the sign it's time to switch tools.
